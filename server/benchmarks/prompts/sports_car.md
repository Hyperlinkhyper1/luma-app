# 3D SPORTS CAR BENCHMARK — STUDIO REVEAL AND TEST DRIVE

Build a polished, interactive 3D sports car experience as a single HTML
page.

This is a visual and technical benchmark. Every model gets the same brief;
the page you produce is compared side by side with other models' pages.
The car's sculpted body is the centre of the judging: it must read as a
real, desirable car, not a box on four cylinders.

==================================================
LIBRARIES
==================================================

Use Three.js r160, loaded with exactly this importmap:

```html
<script type="importmap">
{"imports":{"three":"https://unpkg.com/three@0.160.0/build/three.module.js","three/addons/":"https://unpkg.com/three@0.160.0/examples/jsm/"}}
</script>
```

Official Three.js addons (OrbitControls, PointerLockControls,
EffectComposer, UnrealBloomPass, Sky, Water, RoomEnvironment, ...) may be
imported from "three/addons/". Nothing else may be loaded from the network:
no external models (GLTF/OBJ/FBX), textures, images, HDRIs, fonts, audio
files or other libraries. Every mesh, texture and sound is generated by your
code (procedural geometry, CanvasTexture / DataTexture, shaders, Web Audio).

CSS goes inside <style>. JavaScript goes inside <script type="module">.

==================================================
THE CAR
==================================================

Design an ORIGINAL mid-engine two-seat sports car. Do not copy a real
model and do not draw any real brand's logo or name — invent your own
marque and put its name and a simple emblem on the car.

The body must be one flowing, organic shell with believable proportions
(about 4.5 m long, 2.0 m wide, 1.15 m tall, cab-forward, long rear deck).
Build it from custom BufferGeometry, lofted cross-sections, lathed or
extruded profiles or a deformed subdivided mesh — never from boxes.

Required details, all modelled in 3D:

- wheels with multi-spoke rims, tyres with a visible sidewall and tread,
  brake discs (drilled or slotted) and coloured calipers seen through the
  spokes
- headlights with projector units and daytime running lights; taillights
  that brighten when braking; amber indicators
- door shut lines, side air intakes behind the doors, front splitter,
  rear diffuser with fins, exhaust tips, side mirrors, door handles
- a glass canopy you can see through, with an interior behind it: two
  bucket seats, a steering wheel, a dashboard with a lit instrument
  cluster, a centre console
- an active rear wing that rises above 120 km/h and drops back below 80

Materials: car paint with clearcoat (MeshPhysicalMaterial), tinted glass,
rubber, brushed and polished metal, carbon fibre (procedural weave
texture). Reflections must come from an environment map generated in code
(PMREM from a procedural studio or the drive environment).

==================================================
MODE 1 — SHOWROOM (DEFAULT)
==================================================

The page opens in a photo studio: dark, reflective floor, soft-box area
lights, a slow turntable rotation and a short reveal animation (lights
coming up across the body, or a cover sliding off).

The user can orbit, zoom and pan. Camera presets: FRONT 3/4, SIDE,
REAR 3/4, TOP, INTERIOR, WHEEL CLOSE-UP.

A configurator panel offers:

- at least 6 paint colours, including one metallic and one matte finish
- rim finish (silver, black, bronze) and caliper colour
- open / close the doors (butterfly or scissor doors, animated on hinges)
- headlights on / off
- an X-RAY / EXPLODED view that fades the body and reveals the chassis:
  mid-mounted engine with intake plenum and exhaust manifolds, gearbox,
  double-wishbone suspension with springs and dampers at each corner,
  radiators in the front, fuel tank, battery, monocoque tub. Label each
  part on hover.

==================================================
MODE 2 — TEST DRIVE
==================================================

A DRIVE button takes the same car onto a closed circuit or coastal road
loop with kerbs, run-off areas, barriers, trees and distant scenery.

Controls: W/S or arrow keys for throttle and brake, A/D or arrows for
steering, Space for handbrake, Q/E for manual gear changes (with an
automatic mode), C to cycle cameras (chase, bonnet, cockpit, trackside TV
camera), R to reset onto the track.

Vehicle dynamics must feel plausible, not like a sliding box:

- engine with an rpm range and a torque curve, a rev limiter, gear ratios
- speed-sensitive steering, grip that falls off when overdriven, oversteer
  when lifting or using the handbrake mid-corner
- weight transfer visible as body roll in corners and pitch under braking
  and acceleration; wheels steer, spin with speed and follow the suspension
- tyre smoke and skid marks on the road when the tyres slide
- engine sound synthesised with the Web Audio API whose pitch and timbre
  follow rpm and load, with a gear-change cut; enable audio on the first
  user interaction and offer a mute toggle

HUD: speedometer (km/h), tachometer with redline, gear, lap timer with
best lap, a minimap of the track with the car's position.

A SHOWROOM button returns to the studio.

==================================================
QUALITY BAR (APPLIES TO EVERYTHING ABOVE)
==================================================

- No placeholders, TODOs, "coming soon" buttons or dead controls. Every
  control on screen does what its label says.
- The scene starts on its own within a few seconds of opening, with a short
  loading state if generation takes time — never a page stuck on "Loading".
- It fills the window and handles resizes. Clamp the device pixel ratio
  (at most 2) so it stays smooth on high-DPI screens.
- Aim for a steady 60 fps on a mid-range laptop GPU. Use instancing, merged
  geometry and level of detail where the object count is high.
- If WebGL is unavailable, show a clear message instead of a blank page.
- Keep the UI compact and legible over the 3D view; it must not cover the
  subject of the scene. Provide a way to hide it.
- Prefer real shapes over boxes: curves, bevels, lathed and extruded
  profiles, custom BufferGeometry. A scene made of plain cubes fails.
