How the signalling works
Red stops the train: the block ahead is occupied, held by an operator, or has no available route.
Yellow permits a cautious approach because the signal beyond is red.
Green means two clear block paths are available.

Each train reserves the next block before it reaches the switch. A switch route is mutually locked, physically selected, and released only after the train’s last car has cleared it. Occupancy is measured at every car bogie, so long trains occupy every block they touch. The two-block single line is one direction-locked resource; opposing trains use the passing loop while it is occupied. Click either lamp on a signal to hold it red.
