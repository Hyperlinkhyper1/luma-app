How the signalling works▸

The line is cut into six blocks. A block is a length of track with a signal at its entrance, and it holds one train at a time. A train occupies every block that any of its vehicles is standing on, so a long freight can straddle two sections at once.

Each signal shows three aspects. Red — the block ahead is occupied, or no route is set. Yellow — the block ahead is clear, but the signal after it is at red. Green — clear for at least two blocks ahead. Trains brake to a stand before a red, ease off for a yellow and run at line speed for a green.

Before a signal in front of turnouts can clear, a route is requested and reserved: the blades swing, then lock while the route is held, and the route is released only once the train has fully cleared the section behind it. Two conflicting routes can never be reserved together, so no train can ever be routed into another.

The north section through the tunnel and over the bridge is single track, worked in both directions. It is reserved as one direction-locked route, and a train may only enter when the section beyond it is also free — which is why trains wait in the passing loop at Ashcombe rather than meeting in the tunnel. That rule is what keeps the layout running indefinitely without deadlock.

Click any signal in the diagram or in the 3D view to place a manual hold; click again to release it. Everything you see is real logic running live — no scripted timings.
