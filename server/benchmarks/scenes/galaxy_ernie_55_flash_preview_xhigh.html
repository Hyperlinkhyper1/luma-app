HOW IT WORKS

Scale architecture. Three independent layers — planet-local (metres), stellar (thousands of km), galactic (tens of thousands of light-years) — each with its own origin and camera. The ship model lives in a local layer that is re-anchored to the active layer; distant layers render with logarithmic depth and are rescaled rather than translated. This avoids z-fighting, jitter and precision loss across ~17 orders of magnitude.

Galaxy. 260,000 GPU points: logarithmic spiral arms, a central bar, a bulge, thin disc and halo, coloured by stellar temperature, with procedural dust extinction and H-II emission. Differential rotation is computed per star in the vertex shader. The black hole lensing is a screen-space deflection of the starfield around the core, over a raymarched-looking accretion torus.

Cinematic. A single continuous timeline moves the ship from a departure world through hyperdrive, past the core, into a nebula and on to a ringed arrival system. Camera and exposure are interpolated; the galaxy and planet layers fade in and out by distance.
