How the signalling works

Every rail section is a block and can belong to only one train. A train reserves the next block before its signal can clear. At a turnout, the selected road is reserved first; the blades move, lock, and release only after the train’s tail has cleared.

The central bridge-and-tunnel section has one direction lock. Trains from either end wait in their station throat until the section and its approach are free. A red signal causes a real brake curve; yellow imposes a cautious approach speed. Click any signal to place a manual hold.
