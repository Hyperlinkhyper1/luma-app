How the signalling works

Blocks: every piece of track is an occupancy block. A block may contain only one train; the freight train therefore has the centre stone bridge as a genuinely single-line, direction-locked section.

Signals: a signal is red when its block is occupied, reserved by another train, manually held, or has no usable route. It is yellow when that block is clear but the block after it is not. It is green when the train has a clear route through at least two blocks.

Routes and points: a train asks for its next block before entering it. At a switch the route is reserved first, the blades move, and the points then lock. The lock is released only after the train has cleared the route. Conflicting point requests cannot be granted together.

No gridlock: the north and south passing routes let trains pass the central freight line. Trains obey red and yellow signals, brake in time, and use passing loops or stations while waiting. A manual signal hold behaves exactly like a real red aspect until it is released.
