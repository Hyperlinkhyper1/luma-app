# BLENDER 3D BENCHMARK — MEGALOPHOBIC CATHEDRAL

Your task is to create a complete 3D scene representing the interior of an enormous fictional cathedral.

The final deliverable MUST be exactly ONE `.glb` file.

This is a benchmark of your ability to:
- reason about 3D space
- design architecture
- model complex geometry
- establish believable scale
- create coherent materials
- compose a scene artistically
- optimize a large environment
- use lighting effectively
- create atmosphere
- solve visual problems independently

Do NOT simply create a normal real-world cathedral.

The cathedral should evoke MEGALOPHOBIA: the uncomfortable feeling of being confronted with structures, spaces, or objects that are impossibly enormous compared with a human.

The most important visual reference is the idea of an impossibly tall, monumental cathedral interior containing gigantic suspended or architectural structures.

Do not directly copy any existing work.
Interpret the concept yourself.


==================================================
CORE EXPERIENCE
==================================================

The viewer should feel tiny.

Imagine a human standing inside a structure built for something hundreds of times larger than themselves.

The cathedral should feel:

- impossibly tall
- ancient
- monumental
- mysterious
- beautiful but disturbing
- sacred, yet unfamiliar
- architecturally coherent
- far larger than something humans would realistically construct

A person looking upward should struggle to understand where the cathedral ends.

The architecture should disappear into darkness, fog, light, or distance.


==================================================
SCALE
==================================================

Scale is one of the most important parts of this benchmark.

Include recognizable scale references somewhere in the environment.

Examples:

- human-sized statues
- doors
- staircases
- railings
- benches
- candles
- balconies
- platforms
- arches
- pillars

These should make the enormous architecture surrounding them feel even larger.

Do NOT achieve scale simply by making an empty giant room.

The scene should contain enough architectural detail at multiple scales that the size feels believable.


==================================================
ARCHITECTURAL DESIGN
==================================================

Design your own cathedral.

You may use ideas from:

- Gothic architecture
- dark fantasy
- monumental brutalism
- impossible architecture
- sacred architecture
- alien architecture
- biomechanical design
- ancient ruins
- monumental machinery

But the final design must feel like one coherent architectural language.

The cathedral should contain multiple layers of architecture rather than just a floor with tall walls.

Consider elements such as:

- gigantic columns
- arches
- vaulted ceilings
- enormous windows
- suspended structures
- bridges
- balconies
- staircases
- towers inside the structure
- galleries
- shrines
- statues
- hanging objects
- structural ribs
- distant platforms

You decide which elements actually belong in your design.


==================================================
THE CENTRAL MONUMENT
==================================================

Create one dominant architectural or sculptural feature inside the cathedral.

This should be one of the things that creates the strongest feeling of megalophobia.

It could be, for example:

- an enormous suspended figure
- an impossible altar
- a gigantic mechanical angel
- a monumental sacred machine
- a colossal statue
- an enormous skeletal structure
- an architectural organism
- something completely original

You decide what it is.

It should feel integrated into the cathedral rather than simply placed inside it.

Its size should initially be difficult to understand.

Only nearby architecture and human-scale objects should reveal how enormous it actually is.


==================================================
VERTICALITY
==================================================

Verticality is extremely important.

The viewer should naturally want to look upward.

Use composition and architecture to pull the eye toward the upper levels.

There should be meaningful geometry far above the viewer.

Do not simply make extremely tall flat walls.

Create architectural layers at different heights.

For example:

GROUND LEVEL
↓
lower arches / halls
↓
balconies
↓
large structural elements
↓
upper galleries
↓
gigantic suspended structures
↓
vaulting / roof structures
↓
architecture disappearing into darkness or atmospheric haze


==================================================
DEPTH
==================================================

The scene should work at three different scales.

NEAR: detailed architecture and objects around the viewer.

MID: columns, arches, stairs, statues, balconies, structural features.

FAR: massive architecture disappearing into distance.

A viewer should be able to look through multiple layers of architecture.


==================================================
LIGHTING
==================================================

Lighting is a major part of this benchmark.

Do not illuminate everything evenly.

Create strong contrast between:

- darkness
- indirect light
- shafts of light
- glowing architectural elements
- silhouettes

Large areas of the cathedral may disappear into darkness.

Possible light sources could include:

- enormous windows
- skylights
- glowing symbols
- suspended lights
- candles
- magical light
- mysterious architectural illumination

You decide what makes sense.

Use lighting to reveal scale.

For example, a beam of light crossing a gigantic column can make the viewer understand its dimensions.


==================================================
ATMOSPHERE
==================================================

The cathedral should contain atmospheric depth.

Possible techniques:

- fog
- haze
- dust
- volumetric lighting
- distant silhouettes
- partially obscured upper architecture

The environment should feel vast enough that the air itself affects visibility.

Avoid excessive fog that simply hides unfinished geometry.


==================================================
MATERIALS
==================================================

Create proper materials.

Do not rely entirely on plain flat colors.

Materials should have appropriate differences in:

- roughness
- metallic properties
- surface variation
- emission where appropriate

Possible materials include:

- stone
- aged stone
- metal
- oxidized metal
- glass
- carved surfaces
- dark polished material
- worn flooring

You decide the cathedral's material language.


==================================================
GEOMETRIC DETAIL
==================================================

Detail should exist at different scales.

MACRO DETAIL: overall architecture.

MEDIUM DETAIL: arches, supports, balconies, stairs, statues.

SMALL DETAIL: carvings, trim, railings, structural connections, paneling, floor details.

Avoid repeating the exact same primitive hundreds of times without meaningful variation.

Repetition is allowed where it makes architectural sense.


==================================================
FLOOR
==================================================

The floor should receive proper design attention.

Do not leave it as a simple plane.

Consider:

- stone slabs
- monumental patterns
- cracks
- channels
- decorative geometry
- elevation changes
- stairs
- reflections
- damaged areas

The floor should help guide the eye toward the major architectural elements.


==================================================
CAMERA / VIEWER AREA
==================================================

Design the environment so there is a strong initial viewing position near human eye level.

Approximate eye height: 1.6–1.8 meters.

From this area, the viewer should immediately perceive the enormous scale of the cathedral.

The scene should still look interesting when explored from other angles.

Do not build only what one camera can see.


==================================================
3D COHERENCE
==================================================

The structure must make reasonable spatial sense.

Avoid:

- accidental floating geometry
- unexplained intersections
- completely disconnected architectural pieces
- paper-thin objects when thickness is expected
- impossible stairs
- random geometry used only to make the scene complicated

Intentional impossible architecture is allowed, but it should clearly appear intentional.


==================================================
GLB REQUIREMENTS
==================================================

The final result MUST be:

ONE FILE: `cathedral.glb`

The GLB must contain everything required to view the scene.

Do not provide:

- separate textures
- separate models
- OBJ files
- FBX files
- Blend files
- multiple GLB files

All required materials and textures must be embedded or otherwise correctly packed inside the GLB.

The model should import correctly into standard GLTF/GLB viewers.


==================================================
OPTIMIZATION
==================================================

This is a large environment, but it should still be reasonably optimized.

Use techniques such as:

- instancing
- modular architecture
- reusable structural elements
- sensible polygon density

Spend geometry where it noticeably improves the scene.

Do not create millions of unnecessary polygons for invisible detail.

At the same time, do NOT sacrifice the cathedral's complexity simply to make the model extremely lightweight.

Balance visual complexity and performance.


==================================================
CREATIVITY RULE
==================================================

You are expected to make significant design decisions yourself.

Do NOT ask the user:

- what the cathedral should look like
- what the central monument should be
- what materials to use
- what colors to use
- what architectural style to use
- how the building should be laid out

Those decisions are part of the benchmark.

Use your own reasoning.


==================================================
WORKFLOW
==================================================

Before finishing, follow this process:

1. Establish the cathedral's overall architectural concept.

2. Block out the main dimensions and test whether the scale feels enormous.

3. Create the main architectural masses.

4. Add the central monumental structure.

5. Add intermediate architecture so the cathedral does not feel empty.

6. Add human-scale references.

7. Add secondary architectural detail.

8. Build materials.

9. Establish the lighting.

10. Add atmospheric depth.

11. Inspect the scene from human eye level.

12. Inspect the scene from several additional viewpoints.

13. Look specifically for:
   - floating geometry
   - broken normals
   - clipping
   - obviously unfinished areas
   - repetitive primitive spam
   - bad scale relationships
   - empty sections
   - weak silhouettes
   - poor lighting
   - unnecessary geometry

14. Correct any problems you find.

15. Re-evaluate the megalophobia effect.

Ask yourself: "Does this actually make a human viewer feel tiny?"

If the answer is weak, improve the scale cues, vertical architecture, central monument, depth, composition, or lighting.

16. Export the complete result as: cathedral.glb


==================================================
PRIORITY ORDER
==================================================

If limitations force compromises, prioritize in this exact order:

1. Correct, valid GLB output
2. Strong sense of gigantic scale
3. Megalophobia
4. Coherent cathedral interior
5. Central monumental structure
6. Strong composition
7. Architectural complexity
8. Lighting
9. Atmosphere
10. Materials
11. Fine detail
12. Optimization


==================================================
FAIL CONDITIONS
==================================================

The benchmark should be considered unsuccessful if the result is primarily:

- a normal-sized cathedral
- a basic Gothic hall
- an empty giant box
- a handful of stretched cubes
- one enormous statue inside an otherwise ordinary room
- flat walls with repeated windows
- random geometry without architectural logic
- a scene where the enormous scale is impossible to perceive
- an exterior instead of an interior
- a render instead of a 3D model
- multiple files instead of one GLB


==================================================
FINAL OUTPUT
==================================================

Produce the finished 3D environment.

The final user-facing deliverable must be exactly one usable file: cathedral.glb
