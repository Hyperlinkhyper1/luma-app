# 3D TRAIN WORLD BENCHMARK — MINIATURE RAILWAY WITH REAL SIGNALLING

Build a living miniature railway world as a single HTML page: a model
railway layout on a tabletop, with three trains running at the same time on
one shared track network, kept apart by a working block-signalling and
interlocking system.

This is a visual AND a logic benchmark. Every model gets the same brief.
The trains must never collide, never pass through each other, never stop
forever in a deadlock, and never teleport — and the user must be able to
see why each train is doing what it does.

==================================================
LIBRARIES
==================================================

Use Three.js r160, loaded with exactly this importmap:

```html
<script type="importmap">
{"imports":{"three":"https://unpkg.com/three@0.160.0/build/three.module.js","three/addons/":"https://unpkg.com/three@0.160.0/examples/jsm/"}}
</script>
```

Official Three.js addons (OrbitControls, PointerLockControls,
EffectComposer, UnrealBloomPass, Sky, Water, RoomEnvironment, ...) may be
imported from "three/addons/". Nothing else may be loaded from the network:
no external models (GLTF/OBJ/FBX), textures, images, HDRIs, fonts, audio
files or other libraries. Every mesh, texture and sound is generated by your
code (procedural geometry, CanvasTexture / DataTexture, shaders, Web Audio).

CSS goes inside <style>. JavaScript goes inside <script type="module">.

==================================================
THE WORLD
==================================================

A miniature diorama on a wooden table or baseboard, with a slight
tilt-shift miniature look (shallow depth of field is optional). It
contains:

- rolling hills with a tunnel through one of them
- a river with a railway bridge (truss or arched stone)
- a small village: houses with roofs and chimneys, a church, trees,
  fences, roads with a few moving cars
- at least one level crossing where barriers lower and red lights flash
  while a train approaches and passes, and road cars stop and wait
- a day / night cycle: at night windows, street lamps, train headlights
  and signal lamps glow

==================================================
THE TRACK NETWORK
==================================================

Lay out a network with real choices, not just one oval. It must include:

- two stations with platforms (one may be a terminus or yard)
- at least four switches (turnouts), visibly modelled, whose blades
  animate when they change
- at least one passing loop where trains can wait for each other
- at least one SINGLE-TRACK section that trains use in both directions,
  so routing through it needs real coordination
- the bridge and the tunnel on the route

Track must be modelled: rails, sleepers and ballast, with smooth curves
(no visible kinks where segments join). Trains follow the rails exactly,
and each car follows the track behind the one in front (bogies on the
curve, not one stiff rod).

==================================================
THE THREE TRAINS
==================================================

Each one distinct and recognisable at a glance:

1. A steam locomotive with tender and coaches: animated driving rods and
   wheels, smoke and steam puffs that follow speed.
2. A modern electric passenger train (multiple unit): pantograph, sliding
   doors that open at station stops, lit interior at night.
3. A diesel freight train with mixed wagons (tank wagon, box van, flat
   wagon with containers, hopper).

Wheels rotate in proportion to distance travelled. Trains accelerate and
brake at believable rates, dwell at stations for a set time, and each has
its own route through the network (it may differ per lap).

==================================================
SIGNALLING AND INTERLOCKING (THE CORE OF THE TEST)
==================================================

Implement it as real logic, not as scripted timings:

- Divide the network into BLOCKS. A block holds at most one train. A train
  occupies every block any of its cars is on.
- Every block entry has a signal with three aspects:
  RED = the block ahead is occupied or no route is set,
  YELLOW = the block ahead is clear but the signal after it is red,
  GREEN = clear for at least two blocks.
- ROUTES: before a signal in front of switches can clear, the route
  through them is requested and reserved; the switches move, then lock
  while the route is reserved; the route releases behind the train.
  Conflicting routes can never be reserved at the same time.
- Trains obey signals: they brake in time to stop before a red signal,
  slow for yellow, and only proceed when the aspect clears.
- The single-track section must never see two trains enter from opposite
  ends: reserve it as one direction-locked route, with trains waiting in
  the passing loop until it is free. The system must run indefinitely
  without deadlock.

Signals are modelled in 3D (posts with lit lamps facing the train) and
must show their real aspect at all times.

==================================================
UI AND INTERACTION
==================================================

- A live 2D schematic track diagram (overlay, toggleable) showing every
  block coloured by occupancy, every signal's aspect, every switch's
  position, reserved routes highlighted, and each train as a labelled
  marker.
- A panel per train: name, speed, current block, next signal and its
  aspect, and state (running, braking for signal, waiting for route,
  dwelling at station).
- Click a signal in 3D or on the diagram to force it to red (manual hold),
  click again to release; trains must respond correctly.
- Click a train to follow it with the camera; a free orbit camera
  otherwise; presets for the station, the bridge, the level crossing and an
  overview.
- Time controls: pause, 1x, 2x, 4x; a day / night slider.
- A toggle that colours the 3D track by block occupancy and shows reserved
  routes.
- A short "How the signalling works" panel explaining the rules above in
  the page's own words.

==================================================
QUALITY BAR (APPLIES TO EVERYTHING ABOVE)
==================================================

- No placeholders, TODOs, "coming soon" buttons or dead controls. Every
  control on screen does what its label says.
- The scene starts on its own within a few seconds of opening, with a short
  loading state if generation takes time — never a page stuck on "Loading".
- It fills the window and handles resizes. Clamp the device pixel ratio
  (at most 2) so it stays smooth on high-DPI screens.
- Aim for a steady 60 fps on a mid-range laptop GPU. Use instancing, merged
  geometry and level of detail where the object count is high.
- If WebGL is unavailable, show a clear message instead of a blank page.
- Keep the UI compact and legible over the 3D view; it must not cover the
  subject of the scene. Provide a way to hide it.
- Prefer real shapes over boxes: curves, bevels, lathed and extruded
  profiles, custom BufferGeometry. A scene made of plain cubes fails.
