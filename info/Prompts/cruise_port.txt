# 3D CRUISE PORT BENCHMARK — A CRUISE SHIP DOCKED IN LISBON

Build an interactive 3D scene of a large cruise ship docked at the cruise
terminal in Lisbon, Portugal, as a single HTML page.

This is a visual and technical benchmark. Every model gets the same brief.
It is judged on the ship, on how recognisable Lisbon is, on the sense of
scale between the ship and the city, and on the life in the port.

==================================================
LIBRARIES
==================================================

Use Three.js r160, loaded with exactly this importmap:

```html
<script type="importmap">
{"imports":{"three":"https://unpkg.com/three@0.160.0/build/three.module.js","three/addons/":"https://unpkg.com/three@0.160.0/examples/jsm/"}}
</script>
```

Official Three.js addons (OrbitControls, PointerLockControls,
EffectComposer, UnrealBloomPass, Sky, Water, RoomEnvironment, ...) may be
imported from "three/addons/". Nothing else may be loaded from the network:
no external models (GLTF/OBJ/FBX), textures, images, HDRIs, fonts, audio
files or other libraries. Every mesh, texture and sound is generated by your
code (procedural geometry, CanvasTexture / DataTexture, shaders, Web Audio).

CSS goes inside <style>. JavaScript goes inside <script type="module">.

==================================================
THE SETTING — LISBON
==================================================

The ship is moored at the Lisbon Cruise Terminal (Jardim do Tabaco, by
Santa Apolónia) on the north bank of the Tagus estuary, below the Alfama
district. Make Lisbon recognisable through its landmarks and character:

- the Alfama hillside rising behind the terminal: dense houses with
  terracotta roofs and pastel or white facades, some covered in tiles
  (azulejos), stepping up the hill along narrow winding streets
- São Jorge Castle on the hilltop, with its walls and towers
- the white dome of the National Pantheon (Santa Engrácia) close to the
  terminal, and the twin towers of the Sé cathedral
- Praça do Comércio's yellow arcades and triumphal arch on the waterfront
  to the west
- the red 25 de Abril suspension bridge spanning the Tagus to the west,
  and the Cristo Rei statue on its pedestal on the south bank (Almada)
- a yellow tram (like the number 28) climbing a street
- the wide Tagus itself, with the south bank visible across the water

You may compress the distances between landmarks so they fit in view, but
keep each one's own scale plausible: the bridge deck is about 70 m above
the water and its towers about 190 m tall; Alfama's houses have 3–5
floors.

==================================================
THE CRUISE SHIP
==================================================

An ORIGINAL modern cruise ship — no real cruise line's name, logo or
livery. About 300 m long, 38 m wide, with around 18 decks above the
waterline. The ship must tower over the terminal and the houses behind it.

Required detail:

- a sculpted hull with a raked bow, a bulbous bow below the waterline, a
  shaped stern, the ship's name on the bow and stern, waterline and
  boot-top colours, anchors in hawse pockets
- rows upon rows of balcony cabins with railings and glass dividers,
  lifeboats and tenders hanging in davits along the promenade deck
- the bridge with bridge wings, a radar mast, one or more funnels
- a top pool deck: pools, water slides, sun loungers, a running track
- mooring lines from the bow and stern to bollards on the quay, and
  gangways connecting the ship to the terminal building
- lit windows and deck lights at night

==================================================
LIFE IN THE PORT
==================================================

- passengers (simple instanced figures are fine) walking between the
  terminal and the gangways, and along the waterfront
- provisioning trucks, a forklift moving pallets, taxis and coaches
  arriving and leaving
- a tugboat standing by, a pilot boat, ferries crossing the Tagus to the
  south bank, a few sailboats
- seagulls circling

==================================================
WATER, LIGHT AND TIME
==================================================

- animated water for the Tagus with reflections of the ship and city,
  small waves, and wakes behind moving boats
- a day / night cycle with Lisbon's warm golden-hour light as a highlight;
  at night the ship, the city, the castle and the bridge light up
- optional weather (clear, haze, light rain)

==================================================
CAMERAS AND INTERACTION
==================================================

- a free orbit camera, plus presets: MIRADOURO (from a viewpoint high in
  Alfama looking over the roofs to the ship and the river, like the
  Miradouro de Santa Luzia), WATERFRONT (at quay level beside the hull, to
  feel its size), AERIAL, FROM THE RIVER (from a passing ferry), and
  SHIP'S DECK (from the top deck looking at the city)
- a walk mode on the quay (WASD + mouse look)
- a time-of-day slider and play / pause for time
- a DEPARTURE button: the horn sounds (synthesised with Web Audio), the
  gangways retract, the mooring lines are released, the tug pushes the
  bow out and the ship slowly turns and sails west down the Tagus towards
  the bridge; a second button brings it back to its berth

==================================================
QUALITY BAR (APPLIES TO EVERYTHING ABOVE)
==================================================

- No placeholders, TODOs, "coming soon" buttons or dead controls. Every
  control on screen does what its label says.
- The scene starts on its own within a few seconds of opening, with a short
  loading state if generation takes time — never a page stuck on "Loading".
- It fills the window and handles resizes. Clamp the device pixel ratio
  (at most 2) so it stays smooth on high-DPI screens.
- Aim for a steady 60 fps on a mid-range laptop GPU. Use instancing, merged
  geometry and level of detail where the object count is high.
- If WebGL is unavailable, show a clear message instead of a blank page.
- Keep the UI compact and legible over the 3D view; it must not cover the
  subject of the scene. Provide a way to hide it.
- Prefer real shapes over boxes: curves, bevels, lathed and extruded
  profiles, custom BufferGeometry. A scene made of plain cubes fails.

============================================================
OUTPUT FORMAT — READ CAREFULLY
============================================================

Reply with ONE complete, self-contained HTML document and nothing else.

- Start your reply with <!DOCTYPE html> and end it with </html>.
- No explanation, summary or notes before or after the document.
- No Markdown code fences.
- No additional files: all CSS and JavaScript go inline in this one page.
- Libraries may only be loaded from the public CDN URLs this prompt allows.
- The page must fill the browser window (no page scrollbars) and start on
  its own when opened. It is shown inside an app's embedded browser.

Your reply is saved byte for byte as the page, so anything outside the
document breaks it.
