# 3D GALAXY BENCHMARK — A SPACESHIP'S VOYAGE ACROSS A SPIRAL GALAXY

Build a cinematic, interactive 3D space journey as a single HTML page: a
spaceship travels across a procedurally generated spiral galaxy, from one
star system to another, past the galaxy's core.

This is a visual and technical benchmark. Every model gets the same brief.
It is judged on beauty, on scientific plausibility, and on how well it
handles the enormous range of scale from a ship to a galaxy.

==================================================
LIBRARIES
==================================================

Use Three.js r160, loaded with exactly this importmap:

```html
<script type="importmap">
{"imports":{"three":"https://unpkg.com/three@0.160.0/build/three.module.js","three/addons/":"https://unpkg.com/three@0.160.0/examples/jsm/"}}
</script>
```

Official Three.js addons (OrbitControls, PointerLockControls,
EffectComposer, UnrealBloomPass, Sky, Water, RoomEnvironment, ...) may be
imported from "three/addons/". Nothing else may be loaded from the network:
no external models (GLTF/OBJ/FBX), textures, images, HDRIs, fonts, audio
files or other libraries. Every mesh, texture and sound is generated by your
code (procedural geometry, CanvasTexture / DataTexture, shaders, Web Audio).

CSS goes inside <style>. JavaScript goes inside <script type="module">.

==================================================
THE GALAXY
==================================================

A barred spiral galaxy rendered with custom shaders:

- at least 200,000 stars as GPU points (sizes and brightness varied), in
  logarithmic spiral arms, a central bar and a bulge, plus a thin disc and
  a faint halo
- star colour by temperature: blue-white young stars along the arms,
  yellow-orange older stars in the bulge
- dark dust lanes along the inner edge of the arms that dim the stars
  behind them, and pink H-II star-forming regions in the arms
- slow differential rotation (inner stars orbit faster)
- a supermassive black hole at the centre with a glowing accretion disc and
  a gravitational-lensing distortion of the stars behind it (a screen-space
  shader approximation is fine)
- at least one large colourful nebula the ship flies through, built from
  layered sprites or ray-marched noise so it looks volumetric

==================================================
THE SPACESHIP
==================================================

An ORIGINAL, detailed procedural spaceship — not a copy of any franchise's
ship. Hull panels and plating, engine nacelles, glowing thrusters with an
exhaust plume, a lit cockpit canopy, antennae or sensor arrays, blinking
navigation lights (red port, green starboard, white strobes). It should
look good at close range in the chase camera.

==================================================
THE VOYAGE
==================================================

The default mode is an automatic cinematic journey (about 2–3 minutes,
then it loops or picks a new route):

1. DEPARTURE: the ship leaves a planet with an atmosphere glow, clouds and
   a moon, orbiting a star with a corona and lens flare
2. CRUISE: it climbs out of the system; the galaxy's disc and arms become
   visible around it
3. HYPERDRIVE: stars stretch into streaks, a warp tunnel, FOV and engine
   effects, then it drops out deep in the galaxy
4. THE CORE: a fly-by of the black hole and its accretion disc
5. THE NEBULA: a pass through the nebula
6. ARRIVAL: it arrives at a different star system with its own planets
   (for example a ringed gas giant)

Scale is the hard part. Going from a ship tens of metres long to a galaxy
100,000 light-years across must be seamless: no z-fighting, no jitter, no
popping. Use techniques such as a logarithmic depth buffer, a floating
origin, or separate scale layers composited together, and explain the
choice in a small "How it works" panel.

==================================================
UI AND INTERACTION
==================================================

- Camera modes: CINEMATIC (default), CHASE, COCKPIT, GALAXY MAP (free
  orbit of the whole galaxy, with the ship's route drawn on it)
- A HUD: current region name (for example "Perseus Arm", "Galactic Core"),
  distance from the galactic centre in light-years, speed (km/s at
  sublight, multiples of c in hyperdrive), ETA to the destination
- A minimap: a top-down view of the galaxy with the ship's position and
  route
- In GALAXY MAP mode the user can click a point in the galaxy to set a new
  destination; the ship plots a course there and flies it
- Play / pause and journey speed controls
- Bloom post-processing is allowed; a synthesised engine hum with the Web
  Audio API is optional (enabled on first interaction, with a mute toggle)

==================================================
QUALITY BAR (APPLIES TO EVERYTHING ABOVE)
==================================================

- No placeholders, TODOs, "coming soon" buttons or dead controls. Every
  control on screen does what its label says.
- The scene starts on its own within a few seconds of opening, with a short
  loading state if generation takes time — never a page stuck on "Loading".
- It fills the window and handles resizes. Clamp the device pixel ratio
  (at most 2) so it stays smooth on high-DPI screens.
- Aim for a steady 60 fps on a mid-range laptop GPU. Use instancing, merged
  geometry and level of detail where the object count is high.
- If WebGL is unavailable, show a clear message instead of a blank page.
- Keep the UI compact and legible over the 3D view; it must not cover the
  subject of the scene. Provide a way to hide it.
- Prefer real shapes over boxes: curves, bevels, lathed and extruded
  profiles, custom BufferGeometry. A scene made of plain cubes fails.
